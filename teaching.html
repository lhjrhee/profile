Teaching

My teaching goal is to equip students to become critical yet compassionate thinkers who can engage confidently with data and emerging technologies while developing their own creativity and professional judgment.

Teaching Philosophy

I believe students learn most meaningfully when they can use digital tools and data to investigate questions that matter to them. My role as an instructor is therefore not simply to teach software or analytical techniques, but to create an environment in which students can develop the confidence to apply those tools independently.

Drawing from my experience in both fashion industry practice and academia, I emphasize the relationship between technical skills, critical judgment, and real-world decision-making. As technologies evolve, I want students to understand not only how to use a tool, but also when it is useful, where it may fail, and how their own expertise remains essential.

Digital Inclusivity

Designing learning resources that support students with different levels of prior digital experience.

Critical Thinking

Encouraging students to question technological outputs rather than treating digital tools as substitutes for professional judgment.

Applied Learning

Connecting analytical and merchandising concepts to students' questions and real-world fashion industry problems.

Teaching in Practice

Supporting Different Levels of Digital Literacy

While teaching TAM 2100 to large online classes of up to 200 students, I observed that difficulties increased whenever students encountered a new software environment. I responded by restructuring instructional videos and developing additional troubleshooting resources so students could independently navigate common barriers while learning Excel, PowerPoint, and Adobe Illustrator.

Teaching With — Not Around — Generative AI

In the Adobe Illustrator module, students first learned foundational techniques such as anchor points and the pen tool before working with generative AI capabilities. They then reflected on the differences between their own work and AI-generated vectors, including the limitations of generated outputs. This activity encouraged students to view AI as something to evaluate critically rather than as a replacement for foundational knowledge.

Making Data Analytics Meaningful

In advanced fashion data analytics courses, I help students translate statistical concepts into questions they can apply to consumer and merchandising problems. Rather than treating statistical procedures as isolated techniques, I emphasize how analytical decisions connect to practical outcomes such as consumer segmentation, advertising, and retail decision-making.
